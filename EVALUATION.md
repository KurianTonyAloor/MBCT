# Steering Evaluation — 300 Held-Out Pairs

Quantitative companion to the LLM-as-a-Judge runs in [`README.md`](README.md). That section reads prose from six prompts; this document measures the same presets on **300 contrastive pairs**, so every claim below has an `n` and a p-value instead of an anecdote.

- **Date:** 21 November 2026
- **Model:** `phi-3-mini-4k-instruct`, bf16, CPU (8 threads), 32 layers, hidden size 3072
- **Calibration:** `artifacts/profiles/mcp_phi-3-mini.json` — layers `[11, 13, 16, 19, 21]`, `max_alpha` 3.0
- **Data:** 300 pairs, 100 per calibrated trait — [`artifacts/datasets/eval_pairs_300.json`](artifacts/datasets/eval_pairs_300.json)
- **Raw scores:** [`artifacts/datasets/eval_results.json`](artifacts/datasets/eval_results.json) · **Aggregates:** [`artifacts/datasets/eval_summary.json`](artifacts/datasets/eval_summary.json)

**Headline:** directness and formality are sign-correct on 68–91% of their own pairs (`p ≤ 4e-4`). **Verbosity is inverted in all three profiles, in both directions.** At directness +3.00 the likelihood effect is the strongest in the table — and the prose judges in README Runs 1 and 6 rated that same setting *less* direct.

---

## 1. What is tested

Four arms per pair, one shared baseline:

| Arm | Injected |
|---|---|
| Baseline | nothing |
| Direct Architect | α = directness **+2.70**, formality **+1.80**, verbosity **−1.20** |
| Friendly Mentor | α = directness **−1.20**, formality **−1.80**, verbosity **+1.50** |
| Brutal Reviewer | α = directness **+3.00**, formality **0.00**, verbosity **−1.50** |
| *Neutral Baseline* | nothing (empty plan) — see §2 |

`empathy` and `initiative` have no calibrated vectors, so they inject nothing and are not tested; Friendly Mentor's 90% empathy setting is inert by construction.

## 2. Method

**Pairs.** Each pair is one scenario with two responses written to differ in exactly one calibrated trait: a **trait-positive** arm and a **contrast** arm. Pairs were machine-generated by the local `phi3` Ollama model through the trait system prompts in `prompt_registry`, then validated by `DatasetValidator`. Scenarios are held out — no topic or phrasing appears in `CURATED_GOLD_SEEDS` or `DEFAULT_SCENARIOS`, so no vector was fitted on them. Directness and formality pairs are length-balanced on purpose (12–18 words per arm) so that "shorter" cannot masquerade as "more direct"; verbosity pairs keep a real length gap (35–45 vs 8–12 words), which is the point of that trait.

**Metric.** For each pair, teacher-force the trait-positive response and take the mean per-token log-probability of its **first 8 tokens**. Repeat under every arm.

```
Δ(profile, pair) = mean log P_steered(positive | prompt) − mean log P_baseline(positive | prompt)
```

in nats/token, so it is length-normalised. Positive Δ means the steered model finds the target-styled answer *more* likely.

**Steering placement matches generation.** The last prompt position and every response position receive `h ← h + α·v`. The product's hook patches only the last prefill position, which would score unsteered response logits; the harness installs its own hooks for that reason.

**Direction agreement** is the discriminating statistic. For a pair belonging to trait `t`, does `sign(Δ)` match `sign(α_t)`? A profile that merely makes *all* text more likely — or that shifts likelihood for unrelated reasons — scores 50% here. Means alone are misleading: a few large wrong-way pairs can produce a positive mean with 30% agreement, which is exactly what happens on verbosity.

**Control.** A fixed neutral 30-token paragraph is scored under every arm to detect global likelihood inflation:

| Arm | Control logP |
|---|---|
| Baseline | −2.0591 |
| Direct Architect | −2.0784 |
| Friendly Mentor | −2.0924 |
| Brutal Reviewer | −2.0979 |

**Neutral Baseline is not re-scored per pair.** It injects nothing, so it is bit-identical to the baseline arm — verified at exactly `0.0000` Δ on a 6-pair smoke set — and is carried as the identity reference, excluded from scoring via `config.evaluated`.

**Statistics.** Two-sided exact sign test on the number of pairs where `sign(Δ) = sign(α)`. `n = 100` per trait and profile.

## 3. Results per profile

| Profile | Mean Δ (nats/token) | Pairs with Δ>0 | Direction agreement | Sign test | Neutral-control Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct Architect | **+0.048** | 72.0% | 58.7% (300) | p = 0.003 | −0.019 |
| Friendly Mentor | −0.041 | 27.0% | 57.0% (300) | p = 0.018 | −0.033 |
| Brutal Reviewer | **+0.106** | 81.7% | 59.0% (200) | p = 0.013 | −0.039 |

Brutal Reviewer's agreement is computed over 200 pairs because its formality alpha is 0 and that trait makes no prediction for it.

The pooled 57–59% understates the result: it averages a working dial with a broken one. The per-trait split is the real finding.

## 4. Results per trait

| Profile | Trait (α) | Mean Δ | Direction agreement | Sign test | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct Architect | directness (+2.70) | +0.055 | **68/100** | p = 4e-4 | ✔ |
| Direct Architect | formality (+1.80) | +0.069 | **78/100** | p = 2e-8 | ✔ |
| Direct Architect | verbosity (−1.20) | +0.022 | 30/100 | p = 8e-5 | ✘ **inverted** |
| Friendly Mentor | directness (−1.20) | −0.025 | 60/100 | p = 0.057 | ~ weak |
| Friendly Mentor | formality (−1.80) | −0.071 | **85/100** | p = 5e-13 | ✔ |
| Friendly Mentor | verbosity (+1.50) | −0.025 | 26/100 | p = 2e-6 | ✘ **inverted** |
| Brutal Reviewer | directness (+3.00) | **+0.176** | **91/100** | p = 3e-18 | ✔ strongest |
| Brutal Reviewer | formality (0.00) | +0.083 | — (no prediction) | — | spillover |
| Brutal Reviewer | verbosity (−1.50) | +0.061 | 27/100 | p = 5e-6 | ✘ **inverted** |

`✘ inverted` means the dial moves the opposite way from its label at a significance well beyond chance: agreement near 30% is 70% wrong-way, not noise.

## 5. Findings

**Directness and formality are real, controlled axes.** All but one non-zero directness/formality dial moved its own pairs in the dialled direction at 68–91% agreement with `p ≤ 4e-4`. The exception is Friendly Mentor's directness (−1.20) at 60%, `p = 0.057` — right sign, below significance. This is the first evidence in this project that a slider change produces a measurable, sign-correct distribution shift rather than a prose impression.

**Verbosity is inverted in both directions, in all three profiles.** A negative alpha (−1.20, −1.50) makes the *long* response **more** likely (70–73% of pairs the wrong way); a positive alpha (+1.50) makes it **less** likely (74% the wrong way). The dial is not inert — it is anti-correlated with its label. This is sharper than the run-by-run reading of "unvalidated in the useful direction" and is the single defect this evaluation found.

**The effect is not monotone in alpha, because the composite vector is renormalised.** Brutal Reviewer at directness +3.00 moves directness pairs **3.2×** harder than Direct Architect at +2.70 (+0.176 vs +0.055), an 11% alpha difference. The controller clamps the summed multi-trait direction to the dominant alpha, so a preset's behaviour is set by its *direction mix*, not by each slider in isolation.

**Cross-trait spillover is measurable.** Brutal Reviewer's formality slider sits at 50% (α = 0) yet its formality pairs move +0.083 with 81% on the positive side. A zeroed dial is not a zero-effect dial inside a multi-trait preset.

**Every profile makes ordinary text slightly less likely.** The neutral control loses 0.019–0.039 nats/token, worst for Brutal Reviewer — the same ordering as its pair gain. Part of what looks like a large effect is the profile pushing hard in general rather than precisely.

**This contradicts the prose runs at high directness, and the contradiction is informative.** Brutal Reviewer's directness dial is the strongest, cleanest effect here (+0.176, 91/100), and in README Runs 1 and 6 judges scored that same setting as *less* direct. The vector reliably moves the model toward directness-**labelled** training text; that text includes "restate the premise and list considerations", which is not what a reader calls blunt. Likelihood of the calibration target is not the behaviour the label names.

## 6. Limitations

- **Likelihood, not prose.** Finding the target-styled answer more probable is necessary for steering, not sufficient. It does not establish that a generated answer reads better. The judged runs remain the only evidence on readability, and they disagree at high alphas.
- **One model, one calibration.** `phi-3-mini-4k-instruct` on CPU against `mcp-phi-3-mini`. The legacy `mcp_phi3` profile and the other cached models were not evaluated.
- **8 scored tokens per response.** A margin over the opening is stable but coarse; it cannot see a trait that only appears deep in an answer, and 8 tokens is short of a full sentence.
- **Pairs are model-generated.** All 300 come from `phi3` Q4_0 and carry occasional generation artifacts (a leaked `Directness:` prefix, a mojibake apostrophe). Both arms of a pair are affected equally so the paired contrast survives, but the pairs are not human-written.
- **`empathy` and `initiative` are untested** — no calibrated vectors exist for them.
- **Single seed set.** Pairs are fixed; a different regeneration would give slightly different margins, though the split between working and inverted dials is far outside sampling noise.

## 7. Reproducing

```bash
python backend/build_eval_pairs.py     # 300 pairs, checkpointed and resumable
python backend/evaluate_profiles.py    # 1,200 forward passes, writes eval_results.json
python backend/evaluate_profiles.py --analyze
```

`build_eval_pairs.py` needs Ollama on `http://127.0.0.1:11434` with `phi3`; `evaluate_profiles.py` runs locally on CPU. Both accept `--limit` / `--pairs` for a quick subset, and results checkpoint every 5 pairs so an interrupted run resumes rather than restarts.

**Cost:** ~102 minutes wall clock on 8 CPU threads (0.17 s/token, no GPU); 4 forwards per pair. Batching does not help — the pass is memory-bandwidth bound (8 sequences 2.66 s/seq vs 2 sequences 2.73 s/seq).
